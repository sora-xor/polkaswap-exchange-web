# Historical execution research data

These development tools obtain hypothetical execution estimates from archived states. They do not replace the deployed GO protocol, qualify a strategy, access a wallet, or submit a transaction.

The existing hourly replay uses next-hour closes. A finer model needs separate evidence for its signal and later quote state. An hourly close or periodic chart snapshot must not be relabeled as an executable quote. The initial capability check obtained a quote and fee estimates at one fixed June 22 state; it established availability at that state, not complete history or profitability. See the [capability report](reports/bots-historical-execution-capability-2026-09-20.md).

## Explicit clocks

`parseIndexedPoolHistoryWithEvidence` in `src/features/bot-trading/pool-history.ts` returns the existing candle history plus one immutable boundary record per accepted candle. The record retains the genesis, denomination, completed hour, and closing/successor block hashes, heights and indexed timestamps in **seconds**. Invalid, duplicate or missing observations have no candle or boundary. `parseIndexedPoolHistory` remains a compatibility wrapper with its original return shape. The additive evidence API is not yet wired into production replay; it must not be described as a change to deployed trading behavior. Exact millisecond timestamps, parent hashes and canonicality require the separate archive reader below, and `arrivalTimeKnown` remains false.

`fetchIndexedBotHistoryWithEvidence(bot, { startAt, endAt })` in `src/features/bot-trading/history.ts` requests this evidence directly; `{ days }` also selects a rolling window. It always uses completed hourly pool observations, with a maximum of 10,000 hours and 100 pages per asset. It copies each returned row before further awaits, pins the same chain, indexer service and initialized network client throughout pagination and the finalized denomination check, and freezes the returned history and aligned boundaries. Missing or rejected hours remain gaps; another call queries again so newly indexed data can appear. It does not cache incomplete results, fall back to USD prices, or infer exact millisecond arrival times. Existing `fetchBotHistory` callers retain their original return shape and behavior.

`scripts/bots/historical-execution-clock.ts` plans and verifies a quote state using milliseconds throughout:

1. Supply the hourly close's exact block and adjacent successor. The close must lie inside the preceding hour; the successor must establish completion within the next hour. Each block retains hash, parent hash, height and timestamp.
2. Supply a fixed 24-hour funded episode and explicit signal-availability, execution-delay and maximum-lag assumptions. There are no default latency values. Record the policy before retrieving execution outcomes.
3. `planHistoricalExecutionClock` preserves the funded start and endpoint. Its assumed decision time is the successor's block timestamp plus the declared signal delay; the quote target follows the declared execution delay.
4. Retrieve the first canonical block at or after that target and its adjacent predecessor. `verifyHistoricalExecutionClock` rejects earlier states, selectively later states, contradictory identities, expired opportunities and post-deadline states. A failed opportunity is retained; the helper does not choose a replacement.

Inputs are copied and frozen. Getters, unexpected fields, invalid hashes, fractional/overflowing clocks and inconsistent hour proofs are rejected before use. The clock checker does not prove canonical ancestry through omitted blocks; the archive reader and collection protocol must verify the requested states against their canonical finalized source.

`scripts/bots/historical-execution-locator.ts` supplies the metadata-only search for step 4. Call `locateHistoricalExecutionBlock(plan, finalizedUpperBlock, readBlock)` with an explicit upper bound and an independently canonical, finalized block reader. It re-reads the closing block, successor and upper bound before bisecting their original bracket. At most 64 unique-height reads are permitted. Missing or contradictory observations end the search; the locator does not retry, change the target or choose another episode. The callback must enforce transport timeouts and cancellation: the locator bounds read count, not wall-clock duration.

The result retains the exact preceding/execution blocks, copied plan, clock result and ordered read provenance. `HistoricalExecutionLocateError.diagnostic` retains failed read provenance. Every sampled block is checked against the other known identities, timestamps and any known parent relationship. Sparse binary search still relies on the caller's canonical, monotonically timed chain attestation; it cannot prove the timestamps or ancestry of unsampled blocks.

**Block timestamps are not historical browser-receipt times.** Availability and execution delays remain explicit assumptions. The returned label is `hypothetical-archived-quote-state`, with `observedFill: false`. Even a complete archive quote cannot prove that a real transaction was included, its output, fees paid, or risk between observations.

`scripts/bots/historical-execution-block-reader.ts` implements the locator's bounded, read-only callback. Initialize `createHistoricalExecutionBlockReader({ finalizedSource, schemaAnchor }, options)` with independently verified source provenance and a historical schema anchor. It checks mainnet genesis and canonical/finalized anchors, derives `Timestamp.Now` as a plain `u64` from archived V14 metadata, and pins the anchor's runtime code hash. Each serial `readBlock(height)` verifies the canonical hash, header and unchanged code hash before decoding the exact eight-byte timestamp. The finalized source may be newer than the schema anchor; queried historical blocks must match the anchor's code hash. An upgrade, missing state or incompatible timestamp layout fails without selecting a replacement anchor.

The adapter permits at most 64 block reads and 272 RPC calls, with limits of 2 MiB per response and 12 MiB in total. The default timeout is 20 seconds per call, including body reads; the maximum configurable timeout is 30 seconds. `evidence()` and `HistoricalExecutionBlockReadError.diagnostic` expose immutable evidence snapshots, including failed RPC diagnostics. The endpoint is the approved historical archive; redirects, credentials, retries and endpoint overrides are disabled. It reads no quotes or reserves, and the frozen quote reader remains a separate operation.

## Historical encoding and costs

Historical calls must use the metadata and runtime version at the requested state. The codec accepts only the observed V14 runtime/transaction profiles 130/130 and 131/131, both declaring the same ordered seven-extension profile ending in standard `ChargeTransactionPayment`. It checks each extension's metadata layout as well as its name; other SDK-recognized aliases or payment extensions are unsupported. The 131 profile was observed through a metadata-only finalized-state read on 2026-09-20, retained in `output/go-history/execution-archive-capability-20260920/extension-profile-131/`. A current-runtime transaction is still not interchangeable with one encoded from the requested historical state. Parent/post-state runtime or metadata differences require rejection until an explicitly supported upgrade-boundary policy exists.

Native network fees are in XOR. Pool fees are already represented in the quoted output. The 0.5% minimum is rounded down in exact integer units; it does not justify charging pool fees again. A public fake-signature envelope is used solely for an unsigned fee estimate with explicit nonce, era and tip assumptions. It is not a real signature or evidence that an account can pay the fee.

The runtime's SCALE `FeeDetails` includes a `tip` field omitted from the SDK's JSON-oriented type. Decode the runtime response with the explicit SCALE layout, require an exact byte roundtrip, and compare inclusion components with `queryInfo.partialFee`. The original failed capability decode and the successful offline revalidation of the same two responses are both retained.

## Bounded archive reader

`scripts/bots/historical-execution-reader.ts` exports `readHistoricalExecutionQuote(request, options)`. Supply an exact block hash and height, an independently verified finalized source anchor and receipt SHA-256, the expected denominator, the KUSD/XOR direction, and the exact input amount in codec units. This is a Node research tool, not a browser API or wallet integration.

The reader uses the approved historical archive at `https://mof2.sora.org/`. It checks mainnet genesis, canonical hashes and a finalized head; compares the requested state's runtime and metadata with its parent; decodes timestamp, denomination, token identities and DEX 0 from that metadata; and obtains the amount-specific quote and both fee responses at the same hash. The usual successful path makes 19 sequential RPC calls. The hard limits are 24 calls, 2 MiB per response and 8 MiB total, with an explicit per-read timeout up to 30 seconds. Redirects, credentials, retries and fallback states are disabled.

Only an explicit null quote produces `historical-quote-unavailable`. Other missing, malformed or inconsistent evidence throws `HistoricalExecutionReadError`, whose bounded diagnostic retains completed and failed RPC evidence. Preserve that diagnostic alongside the frozen request. Late responses after cancellation cannot modify it. Raw evidence contains public market values; avoid printing it or making held-out observations available while developing a strategy.

The success label is `hypothetical-historical-execution-estimate`. Its quote is amount-specific, its fee envelope uses a public fake signature, and `observedFill` and `transactionSubmitted` are both false. Finality is RPC-attested, not independently proven by a light client. A caller-supplied receipt hash is provenance to verify upstream, not proof by itself. The caller must separately verify the execution clock and retain the complete protocol; the reader does not infer either from the quote.

## Same-state direct pool marks

`scripts/bots/historical-execution-pool-codec.ts` is a separate offline codec; it leaves the frozen quote reader and encoding proof unchanged. `createHistoricalExecutionPoolCodec(identity)` first validates the explicit genesis, block hash, V14 metadata and supported runtime profile using the existing historical codec. A private ownership map lets its pool-layout builder reuse that exact validated registry and metadata for the same codec, avoiding a second metadata decode without sharing state across blocks. Its `storageKeys()` returns the five existing identity/timestamp/denomination keys plus `PoolXYK.Properties(XOR, KUSD)` and `PoolXYK.Reserves(XOR, KUSD)`, derived from that same historical metadata. Fetch all seven raw values at the exact bound hash before calling `decodeStorage`.

The approved historical metadata declares both pool entries as double maps with `Blake2_128Concat` keys. Raw `Properties` storage is a 64-byte `(AccountId32, AccountId32)` tuple (reserves account, fees account). Its query modifier is Optional, but raw `state_getStorage` returns the tuple without an Option tag. Raw `Reserves` storage is a 32-byte `(u128, u128)` tuple in XOR-first order. Its Default modifier does not authorize replacing a null raw response with zero reserves. The codec checks layouts, lengths and exact SCALE roundtrips. Each map key component must encode and roundtrip the exact 32-byte asset code, and the complete generated key must match an independently constructed double-map key; same-arity coercions to other types are rejected.

The decoder preserves `absent` (both entries null), `missing-reserves` (properties present, raw reserves null) and `zero-reserves` (an explicitly encoded zero on either side), with no mark for those states. Reserves without pool properties are contradictory and rejected. Same-state asset records must still identify 18-decimal XOR and KUSD, the denominator must be positive, and the DEX base must be XOR.

A positive pool observation returns exact u128 reserve strings and reciprocal rational marks: `xorPerKusd = xorCodec / kusdCodec`, and `kusdPerXor = kusdCodec / xorCodec`. No rounded price is substituted for these ratios, and KUSD is not pegged to USD. The optional `roundHistoricalPoolRatioTo18(ratio)` performs explicitly requested floor rounding for display, using integer arithmetic and a `below-18-decimal-resolution` status when a positive ratio rounds to zero. Callers must choose valuation rounding explicitly and retain the raw ratio. Denomination is retained as identity evidence rather than silently rescaling one side of the equal-decimal pair.

These cached reserve ratios are pool spot observations. They do not show executable output, network fees, price impact, actual fills or a usable wallet balance. Same-block RPC provenance and finality remain the reader's responsibility; the offline codec does not prove the origin of supplied bytes.

## Before any GO integration

### Batched pool observation reader

`scripts/bots/historical-goal-market-reader.ts` exports `createHistoricalGoalMarketReader({ source, expectedDenominator }, options)`. The source is the same explicit finalized anchor and historical schema anchor used by the metadata block reader. `readBlock(height)` supplies canonical metadata; `readMark(height)` returns `{ block, poolEvidence, mark? }`. A mark exists only for a positive same-state direct pool. Null, missing and zero reserves preserve their explicit statuses.

Each reader is a bounded shard: at most 64 unique block reads and 64 pool reads. The existing block reader checks canonical hashes, full headers, the pinned runtime code hash and exact timestamps. The market reader reuses its retained, hash-verified archived metadata to derive all seven storage keys, and fetches them together with `state_queryStorageAt(keys, blockHash)`. It requires exactly one changeset for that block, every requested key exactly once and no extra keys. It decodes timestamp, denomination, both token records, DEX 0, pool accounts and reserves from that same block. These values are not reused across distinct blocks merely because the runtime is unchanged.

One uncached mark needs four block RPC reads and one storage RPC, plus the shard's ten initialization calls. Storage responses are bounded to 128 KiB each and 8 MiB total, in addition to the block reader's existing 12 MiB budget. Whole-request deadlines cover response bodies. Redirects, credentials, retries and endpoint overrides remain disabled. A failure stops that shard and preserves its evidence; a transport that completes after cancellation cannot mutate the diagnostic, and its late body is cancelled. A complete collection must predeclare its total shard/read budgets and compare source/schema identity between shards.

The fixed June 22 09:03 capability check used one storage batch and reproduced the complete independently retained pool projection exactly. It read no alternative state, emitted no market values and ran no strategy. See the [capability receipt](../output/go-history/goal-replay-development-20260920/market-verification.json).

### Development episode components

`scripts/bots/historical-goal-schedule.ts` exports `planHistoricalGoalSchedule(signals, clock, episode, valuationPolicy)`. Supply all 24 independently verified completed-hour signals in the fixed 24-hour episode, the explicit execution clock policy, and a valuation cadence/maximum lag selected before observing outcomes. A one-minute cadence retains 1,441 requests, including the opening and final endpoints. Those two marks require the exact endpoint timestamp; missing endpoint evidence makes the episode incomplete rather than shifting its funding or end. Intermediate targets select the first canonical block at or after each target within the declared lag. The final execution window cannot extend to the deadline. The consumer must merge **resolved block times**, not merely planned target times, before replay. Valuation continues after trading stops.

`scripts/bots/historical-goal-ledger.ts` supplies pure, immutable accounting with `createHistoricalGoalLedger`, `markHistoricalGoalLedger`, `assessHistoricalGoalFill` and `applyHistoricalGoalFill`. It currently supports the requested development configuration: at most 10 KUSD starting capital, a separate 1 XOR fee allocation, partial orders, 18-decimal KUSD/XOR, a 24-hour goal, 5% target and peak drawdown, 1% impact policy and 0.5% slippage. The opening mark must match the fixed start exactly and the input strategy state must be fresh. Indicator warmup and consumed-signal state belong to the ordered driver.

Valuation remains an exact ratio in XOR codec units: for KUSD/XOR holdings `K,X` and same-state reserves `RK,RX`, value is `(K*RX + X*RK)/RK`. Opening value, observed peaks, target and drawdown comparisons use integer cross-products. No rounded display price enters a risk decision. The ledger reuses the existing exact allocation and fill functions; inputs and network fees debit before proceeds, and only paid fees reduce the protected fee allowance. Admission tests both the quoted-minimum success and fee-only failure against the loss limit without saving projected progress. Applying either explicit hypothetical scenario charges its network fee once. Stops latch, preserve holdings, and still permit subsequent valuation through the endpoint; the independent performance peak/drawdown continues after stopping. The ledger exposes serializable results but currently accepts only states created by that reducer instance; it is not a persistence/restoration API.

`src/features/bot-trading/quote-impact.ts` exports `isExactInputQuoteWithinImpactLimit`. It compares `(amountWithoutImpact - amountOut) / amountWithoutImpact` to the decimal cap using exact integer multiplication. Both values must come from the same exact-input quote and already include pool fees. The helper neither deducts those fees again nor incorporates native fees or slippage into price impact. Invalid or contradictory amounts throw, while a valid over-limit quote returns false. The SDK's default signed display rounding was checked and is conservative; no existing live rounding bypass is claimed.

`scripts/bots/historical-goal-quote.ts` exports `prepareHistoricalGoalFill` to join the frozen proposal amount, verified execution clock, existing archive quote/fee projection and same-state pool projection. It requires matching state, denomination, runtime and metadata identity; DEX 0/XYK and a direct KUSD/XOR route; the exact 0.5% minimum; and a native fee decoded again from retained SCALE fee details. Exact impact above 1% returns an explicit rejection with no replacement order. Missing quote/pool states remain unavailable. A ready result provides a detached `PaperFill` and reserve mark for the ledger; it does not apply a fill. The historical fee policy remains a nonce-zero, tip-zero, immortal-envelope estimate. This differs from the live mortal signing envelope and must remain an explicit scenario assumption until the production policy is reconciled.

`scripts/bots/historical-goal-signals.ts` supplies the sequential completed-hour reducer described in [signal usage](bots-historical-goal-signals.md). It freezes exact proposed inputs before later quote lookup, including inventory-limited sell amounts, and preserves rejected/stopped opportunities in the 24-hour sequence.

### As-of terminal accounting

The original schedule above retains its exact-timestamp contract and original receipts. Blockchain block times need not equal an arbitrary wall-clock deadline. A separately bound development protocol can replace only that terminal selection with `planHistoricalGoalTerminal(episode, maximumAgeMs)` and `verifyHistoricalGoalTerminalClock(policy, observed, successor)` from `scripts/bots/historical-goal-terminal.ts`.

This policy selects the latest canonical state at or before the unchanged deadline `T`, proved by `observed.timestampMs <= T < successor.timestampMs` and adjacent height/parent identities. The freshness bound must be frozen before reading terminal market values; the current development protocol uses 12 seconds, matching the previously declared valuation tolerance. The successor supplies metadata proof only, never terminal prices. Canonical/finalized provenance is still the reader's responsibility. This is explicitly a retrospective chain-state valuation with unknown historical browser arrival time.

Pass a reserve mark matching the proof's **observed block hash and original timestamp**, together with `{ accountingAtMs: proof.accountingAtMs, maximumAgeMs: proof.policy.maximumAgeMs }`, to `finalizeHistoricalGoalLedger`. Keep the observed timestamp separate from the fixed accounting deadline. An active goal expires at the deadline; a terminal-only observation cannot fabricate an earlier target/loss stop. Earlier stops and all holdings, paid fees and exact performance accounting remain intact. The returned terminal object cannot be passed to ledger trading functions. The driver must retire its earlier active reference and finalize once; immutable references are not globally revoked.

The original June 22 development day passed this bounded metadata check: its latest terminal state precedes the unchanged deadline by six seconds. No market values or strategy outcomes were read to establish this. The fixed day was not shifted, and no missing valuation or execution observations were filled in.

The [ordered development replay](bots-historical-goal-replay.md) now connects these components, merges actual event times, preserves all 24 signal opportunities, freezes exact order inputs and values both retained inventory and the unchanged funded benchmark through terminal accounting. It accepts only a complete fixed input dataset or reports explicit incompleteness. A replay result is not a qualification certificate.

A complete historical episode dataset, a new qualification certificate and a browser provider still require integration. Upstream archive readers remain responsible for canonical RPC/finality/metadata evidence. The final integration must bind the route, clock, valuation basis, fee-envelope assumptions, terminal policy and complete evidence digest to the proposed strategy and live controller; existing v2 certificates or exact-endpoint receipts cannot be relabelled as a different protocol.

A separately reviewed protocol must bind the exact strategy, assets, allocation, fee reserve, order ceilings, cost assumptions, clocks and predetermined episodes. Preserve 10 KUSD maximum starting capital, partial orders, the separate 1 XOR reserve, the existing 5% loss limit, 1% impact cap and 0.5% slippage. Preserve missing/rejected observations, the three-candidate ceiling and untouched validation. Current execution quotes and funding checks remain necessary even when historical costs are available.

No observed return, strategy qualification, finalized trade or successful trading recording follows from installing or testing these data tools. The original frozen prospective collector and its development dataset remain unchanged.
