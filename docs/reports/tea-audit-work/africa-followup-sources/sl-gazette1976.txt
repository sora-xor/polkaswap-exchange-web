                                                           CONSUMER Price INDEX, FREETOWN—4TH QuaRTER, 1975


           The revised Freetown         Consumer        Price Index for the 4th quarter of 1975 was 208.0 as compared                                            to 212.7 in the      3rd     quarter
 1975.      This shows a drop of 4.7 points or 2.2 per cent over the                                      previous quarter

        The most important drop among the major proup items was for “Food and Drinks” which dropped by 8.5 per cent over the
 previous period. The index (1961 = 100) indicates the average movement of retail prices of selected item known as “market basket”
 which represent the purposes of the low income African families in Freetown.

         Food and Drinks: A substantial drop of 21.1 points was noted mainly accounted for overall relative price declines in the average
 prices of dried snapper, fresh fish, rice, onions, pounded groundnut and beef (steak). These drops were however partially offset by
 increases in the average prices of dired bonga, big and small peppers, potatoe leaves, fresh tomatoes, coffee, groundnut oil, oranges,
 mangoes and bananas.     The last 3 items were not in season and this might be responsible for their relative price increases.

       Housing: The housing index rose by 8.8 points or 4.9 per cent over the previous quarter. This increase was due to rise in the
average prices of putty, chairs, mattresses, drinking glasses and galvanised kettle (bucket). These rises were probably due to the
period of the year in question.

        Clothing: Like the housing index, clothing index rose substantially by 25.1 points   or 19 percent. The increases were attributed
to a rise in the average prices of shirting, cotton prints, ladies nylon slips, ladies plastic sandals, men’s cotton singlets, cotton drill,
poplin broadcloth, cotton print and tailor charges for men’s and Jadies garments.

        Miscellaneous: The index for this group rose highly by 27.4 points or 18-8 per cent consequent to substantial increases in the
 average prices of bus fares and religious dues.
