# Independent review of Africa findings — 2026-09-29

Reviewed all 11 non-unknown rows in africa.json against available primary text. No mandatory status change identified in ten rows. Recommend Mayotte be `not_verified` until the specific postal-scope and amendment questions below are resolved; this does not establish a ban on manufactured tea.

- BF: The ministry compendium explicitly includes dried leaves and transformed products in the controlled list. Permit interpretation is supported; the remaining processing-exception question is appropriate.
- CD: Decree article 27 expressly covers plant-origin food, requires a permit, and permits phytosanitary OR origin documentation. Article 30 explicitly covers private baggage and post. Current wording preserves that distinction.
- KE: The official Port Health table expressly names tea, Tea Board permission and KEPHIS import permits; separate ordinary manufactured-food row does not override the tea row. Retain the request for an actual personal-use waiver or issued permission.
- LR: WTO paragraphs 3.95 and 3.96 respectively name processed-food import permits and separate personal-use phytosanitary fees. They support the permit route; obtaining the consignment conditions remains necessary.
- MR: Article 8 expressly covers postal consignments and exempts personal food products up to 10 kg. Annex II separately names tea leaves for consumption. The net/gross caveat is appropriate.
- MU: Current NPPO page expressly covers personal as well as commercial use, specifically names green/black tea with NAPRO approval, and states origin phytosanitary documentation. Strong support.
- SC: Plant-product definition includes goods manufactured wholly or partly from plants; section 68 covers permit applications before shipment. Its subsection 5 is a beneficial-organism exception, not a food exception. Section 74 is a discretionary written exemption, not an automatic retail-tea waiver. Retain permit route.
- SH: St Helena official guidance supports sealed commercial preserved food; Ascension explicitly says unlisted types have no additional requirements and its controls describe live plants/fresh produce. Conditional status appropriately preserves unresolved Tristan jurisdiction.
- ST: Article 22 expressly includes dried plants/parts; its small-quantity medicinal/pharmacy exception does not cover ordinary food tea. Articles 23–24 and 38 support prior permission and postal coverage.
- TN: Article 5(6) is a general small-personal-consumption exemption, excluding seeds. It is not textually confined to baggage and has no numeric ceiling. Current conditional status and refusal to assert 24.9 kg clearance are appropriate.
- YT: Base Order 2025-DAAF-0230 Annex II expressly excludes dry tea from the Camellia prohibition; Annex IV chapter 09 identifies unroasted coffee and fresh pepper, not ordinary tea. Targeted search-index extraction resolved amendment 2025-DAAF-535: articles 1–3 only temporarily relaxed Annex III point 8 fresh-fruit Tephritidae conditions until 31 December 2025. Amendment 2026-DAAF-003 changes Annex III point 9 fresh vegetables. Neither changes the tea finding. However, the current official import page (updated 3 June 2026) lists a further amendment 2026-DAAF-236 and a consolidated order that could not be retrieved. More importantly, the separate postal prohibition 2019-1092 Article 1 describes fresh plant material including refrigerated or dry material and plant parts. July 2026 public guidance summarizes it as fresh plant products, but does not explicitly exempt manufactured tea. The older operative wording must not be silently replaced by a supposed unprocessed-only exception. Ask DAAF whether sealed steamed/dried Camellia sinensis food tea is outside the postal restriction, and verify the 2026 consolidation. Do not equate its exclusion from the general species prohibition with permission to use post.
