 Introduction
  Based on the Saudi Food and Drug Authority's system issued under the
  esteemed Cabinet Resolution No. (31) dated 24/1/1428H, ratified by Royal
  Decree No. (M/6) dated 25/1/1428H, the authorization for clearing imported
  food, pharmaceutical products, medical devices, pesticides, and everything
  related to the Authority's scope of work, after conducting the necessary
  inspections and analyses, falls within the executive tasks assigned to the
  Authority.


      This guide has been prepared to assist individuals involved in the food
      sector in understanding the requirements and conditions for food
      clearance, as well as the procedures followed, in order to facilitate and
      expedite the completion of their transactions. It aims to prevent rejection,
      delays, or losses that may arise from a lack of knowledge of these



 Scope
  All food imported for commercial, non-commercial, industrial, and
  consumption purposes.


 Objective
  To clarify the conditions and requirements for food clearance through
  customs ports.
 Definitions:

  The Authority:
  The Saudi Food and Drug Authority.

  Food:
  Anything intended for human consumption as food or drink, whether fresh,
  processed, semi-processed, or raw. It also includes milk and any substance
  used in the manufacturing, preparation, or processing of food.

  Clearance:
  The decision issued regarding approval or denial of imported shipments
  through customs ports.

  Health Certificate:
  A document (paper or electronic) issued by the competent authority in the
  country of origin or export country, indicating the safety and suitability of the
  exported food according to the requirements of relevant technical
  regulations. It may include the registration number of the food establishment
  or alternative equivalent measures that are approved.

  Halal Slaughter Certificate:
  A certificate accompanying shipments of meat, poultry, and their products,
  containing information such as the slaughter date, expiry, etc.

  Halal Certificate:
  A document certifying that a product, service, or systems comply with halal
  requirements in Islamic law, including certificates for halal slaughter, halal-
  certified establishments, farms, slaughterhouses, and facilities, as well as
  certificates for primary product ingredients, additives, and components that
  include meat derivatives, rennet, gelatine, animal fats, oils, and their
  derivatives or any product that requires a halal certificate according to
  technical regulations.

Technical Regulation:
A mandatory document that describes the characteristics of food or its
production and manufacturing methods, including the regulations governing
it. It also includes terms, symbols, packaging, or labelling of the product or its
production method.

Certificate of Conformity:
Third-party certification for products, operations, systems, or individuals.


Port of Entry:
The location where the shipment arrives and is cleared for entry into the
Kingdom.


Proficiency Test (PT):
A proficiency test based on inter-comparison used to assess the performance
of participants (which may include laboratories for inspection and calibration,
inspection authorities, or individuals) in specific tests or measurements, and
to monitor their ongoing performance.
 Mandatory General Requirements for Food Clearance:

Before importing food, the importer must ensure that:

   The imported food complies with the approved technical regulations and the
    circulars published on the Authority's website.
   The importer has registered their establishment on "Ghad" platform with the
    Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA).
   The importer has registered the food items and products they wish to import
    on "Ghad" platform.
   Clearance requests are submitted via the Authority's electronic systems.
   Clearance requests are submitted through the "Fasah" trade platform.
   A licensed warehouse is available in accordance with the Authority's
    requirements. (Exemptions apply for local factories importing raw materials
    that are used in manufacturing their products, as well as importers whose
    total number of shipments per calendar year does not exceed 5 shipments,
    with a combined weight not exceeding 2,000 kg).

 Conditions for Clearing Non-Commercial Products:
  First: Personal Use Products
   Ensure the shipment is not displayed in local markets and is used only for
     its intended purpose.
   The importer is fully responsible for the safety and security of the imported
     products.
  Second: Imported Food Samples for Scientific Studies and Research (Not for
  Human Consumption)
   A letter must be submitted via the "Fasah" platform detailing the imported
     samples and their intended use, along with a commitment from the
     establishment/importer not to sell or display them in local markets.
   A copy of the research/study approval from the regulating authority must
     be provided.
   A commercial invoice and bill of lading must be submitted.
   In the case of research samples, proof must be attached showing the
     samples are for scientific research, provided by the institution to which the
     researcher belongs.
  Third: Display and Marketing Samples Not for Sale
   The labels of food products must include the phrase 'Free Sample'.
   A commitment must be submitted via the "Fasah" platform from the
     establishment/importer ensuring the samples are only for the intended
     purpose and will not be sold in local or international markets after the
     intended use is completed.
   Import of display and marketing samples from animal-origin products is
     only allowed from accredited establishments, and approval must be
     obtained from the authority by sending a request via email at
     CEC@SFDA.GOV.SA before importation.
Fourth: Requirements for Clearing Proficiency Test (PT) Samples and
Standard Materials for Food Product Analysis
 A copy of the invoice from the manufacturer or packager indicating the
    quantities and the name of the proficiency test being sent.
 A copy of the letter from the requesting authority, detailing the request for
    the samples or standard materials to be cleared, or any document proving
    the same.
 A copy of the bill of lading.
 All of the above documents should be submitted via the "Fasah" platform
    (Tabadul) and referred to the authority to complete the clearance
    procedures.
Fifth: Imported Food Samples for Analysis in private laboratories within the
Kingdom
 A letter from the requesting authority for analysis must be submitted via
    the "Fasah" platform (Tabadul) detailing the products to be cleared and the
    purpose of importation, along with the name of the laboratory.
 A copy of the invoice must be provided.
 Only the specified quantities for analysis are allowed to be imported.
 The laboratory must be accredited by the SFDA.
 A commitment from the accredited laboratory must be submitted on the
    "Fasah" platform (Tabadul) stating that the imported products will not be
    displayed, sold, or used in any way other than for analysis purposes.
    Additionally, any remaining samples must be destroyed after completing
    the required analyses.

 Food Products for Manufacturing Purposes, Including Factories and
  Restaurants
  These include food products not intended for direct display or sale to consumers
  but imported for the following purposes:
   Manufacturing in licensed food factories in the Kingdom.
   Restaurants, where the imported food product will not be served or consumed
    directly by the consumer and will undergo processing/preparation. (The
    exception applies only to products used within food preparation areas and does
    not include products displayed on the consumer's table, such as sauces and
    flavourings.)
  Requirements:
   The materials used for manufacturing purposes must comply with the approved
    technical regulations.
   These products are exempt from clause 1.2.7 of the "SFDA.FD/GSO 9" Technical
    Regulation (Labelling of Pre-packaged Food Stuffs) which states: "The labels of
    food products and their accompanying information must be in Arabic, and if
    another language or languages are used alongside Arabic, all data in the other
    languages must match the Arabic version." An exception allows submission of a
    commitment via the Fasah platform that the label will be in English.
  Mandatory Technical Regulations (Specifications Store):
  These regulations are updated regularly, and it is essential to review the updates before
  importing food products.
