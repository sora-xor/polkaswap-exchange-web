# Bot trading

Open `/#/bots` from the main navigation. The workspace is part of the static Polkaswap bundle and also works at `/ipfs/<cid>/#/bots`. It creates no server process, additional wallet, seed phrase, or recovery key.

`/#/bots` opens with the **Strategy Lab** command center. Its self-improving research loop tests 672 rule strategies per SORA market on the bundled hourly pool archive, using exact constant-product fills and current finalized fees. It deploys only markets whose blind walk-forward folds made money. **Go live** opens the existing funding review and a one-glance consent summary. **Paper trade** saves an idle paper bot. **Swap** opens the Swap form prefilled. See [Quant Loop](bots-quant-loop.md) for the protocol, results, capacity limits and tests.

**Start AI trading** is the default beginner flow: connect an AI, set a funded-wallet budget, let the app generate/test a strategy, then unlock once for automatic signing. The user does not select experiments or tune indicators. See [automatic AI trading](autopilot.md) for the workflow and session boundaries. **Advanced** opens the existing research workspace. There, **Create goal** opens the Jev paper-trading setup with a target, loss pause threshold, and time horizon. See [Jev trading goals](jev-goals.md) for connection requirements, progress accounting, and session behavior.

The workspace keeps forms, actions, and results visible. Open **Details** for strategy explanations, goal accounting, and composer help; **Test quality** and **Compare experiments** expand the optional research settings. Pause reasons and trading errors remain visible.

## Discover and approve live strategies

**Discover** searches every directed pair formed from the current eligible token list. A run freezes the exact input and output asset addresses and the latest 90 completed days: 76 days for training, followed by a sealed 14-day chronological holdout. Reversing the addresses makes a different pair; symbols alone do not identify a market. Pairs without complete denomination-verified history, valid directional fee evidence, or a usable route remain visible with a skip reason. Fees are current finalized estimates applied to past prices, not records of past fees. Research capital is a hypothetical amount of the input token **per directed pair**; it is not a wallet deposit or one shared budget across the search. Keep the browser tab and computer active during research and live execution; a suspended page pauses trading.

Before a run, the ready preview shows the possible directed-market count from the current eligible assets and the fixed 76-day training / 14-day holdout split; it shows no research performance or coverage claim. The phase rail separates market screening, AI requests, and holdout evaluation. The progress bar counts **screened** markets, while the animated coverage ring separately shows verified, skipped, and pending directed markets. A scan time estimate appears only after enough progress has been observed in the current page; until then the remaining market count is shown. Skip reasons are grouped by count, and the full per-pair list remains inspectable. The timeline shows the frozen training and holdout boundary; the risk and return chart plots candidate training results, the user's drawdown limit, and holdout points only after their one-time evaluation. The current finalized fee quote assumption appears beside the result chart and candidate returns. Chart markers can be clicked or activated by keyboard to inspect the strategy rule and exact training and completed holdout metrics beside the chart. Keyboard activation moves focus to the inspector's **Show** action, which opens the matching evidence row; inspecting never selects a bot for live review. Nearby markers separate into distinct hit targets with tethers to their exact plotted coordinates. Empty charts contain no synthetic performance data, and reduced-motion settings turn off animation.

Choose OpenAI, Claude, Jev, a versioned custom HTTPS provider, or a paired local Codex or Claude Code companion. Each AI system receives at most 202 recent completed training candles and prior training summaries for one exact directed pair. It returns one typed deterministic strategy per round. The browser tests the fixed rule across the full training window, enforces the user-set request cap (12 by default, at most 36), and stores a credential-free checkpoint so research can pause and resume after the AI connection is restored. Every dispatched request counts, including failed ones; a paid API provider may charge separately. Progress identifies the current pair and research step, the next eligible request time during a cooldown, and used, failed, and remaining requests. A restored checkpoint shows its saved provider type, model ID when available, and the canonical custom HTTPS host when applicable. It stores no endpoint path, API key, or companion pairing token. Changing a connected custom endpoint disconnects it, and resuming on another host asks for explicit confirmation. It ranks candidates before exposing at most three to the holdout. An interrupted holdout exposure makes that candidate exploratory. Before **Start research** or **New search** would overlap a previously exposed holdout, Discover shows the earlier dates and warns that the new results are exploratory and cannot approve live bots. The warning appears before another AI request or checkpoint deletion. Clearing the checkpoint does not erase a holdout exposure. A visible holdout result is never sent back to AI as untouched evidence. The provider contract and local setup are described in [AI discovery providers](ai-discovery-providers.md).

Qualified results can be selected for one fixed live campaign of up to three bots. The comparison and review preserve the order in which the user selected them. Holdout metrics appear in candidate rows and comparisons only after the full finalist evaluation completes; an interrupted exposure remains sealed in those views. Candidate rows put the absolute after-cost return first, then excess over holding, trade count, and drawdown. An absolute loss remains marked as a loss even when it lost less than holding. Typed entry and exit rules are separated for inspection. If no candidate qualifies, a prominent completed outcome shows the failed gates with observed values, the actual holdout exposure state, a new-search action, and a way to inspect skipped pairs. The full provider setup collapses after completion, leaving the evidence and next action in the main workspace. The review shows each pair's exact input and output addresses, rule, studied allocation, input and output order ceilings, XOR fee reserve, chosen drawdown limit, and a shared XOR-equivalent capital cap. The rule, allocation, order ceilings and fee reserve are displayed as fixed study values; the chosen drawdown limit is checked against the sealed holdout. Approval freezes the reviewed bots and limits. Only the built-in wallet can grant unattended signing, and that grant stays in memory for at most 14 calendar days. It ends on interruption or expiry; a user must review and unlock again to continue. The browser and computer must remain active, so a 14-active-day goal may span several grants.

Before the detailed funding review, selected finalists appear in a side-by-side comparison with each request's strategy and fixed rule, actual training and completed holdout return, excess over holding, trade count, and drawdown. Live campaign cards show authenticated active days, finalized swaps, and marked drawdown against the approved limit as bounded progress lanes; the displayed amounts retain their actual values when a bar reaches its end. A 0% drawdown limit is permitted and pauses a bot on its first measured decline. A paused campaign explains when the built-in wallet must be connected before review and unlock can resume it.

Use **Check funding and limits** to inspect the current finalized block, exact shared cap, and required and available token balances before entering the wallet password. This funding check lasts 60 seconds. A visible countdown marks its expiry; **Refresh check** obtains fresh finalized evidence while retaining the finalist selection and entered shared cap, and asks for fresh consent and wallet unlock. The live campaign panel shows each bot's authenticated active days, finalized swaps and goal outcome. **Pause campaign** revokes its shared signing grant. **Review and resume** obtains a new finalized funding review with the same countdown and asks for a fresh wallet unlock; the approved pair, strategy, allocation and limits cannot be changed there.

The review also shows the input and output token order ceilings enforced by the live executor. After pausing or completing a campaign, **Close campaign** asks for a second confirmation, reconciles pending receipts, stops all members and releases their reserved allocations. Closed campaigns cannot be resumed; their bots and orders remain available for audit.

Live results stay in the app unless the user separately selects **Share aggregate live results with this AI for this search** and chooses a bot. The app then sends only its public pair and aggregate active time, finalized swap count, marked return above opening and holding, drawdown and paid XOR fees. A run informed by live results is labeled exploratory and cannot certify finalists against an untouched holdout. The opt-in does not carry across a page reload; resuming a saved run without it removes the pending live summary before another AI request.

Active time accumulates only between consecutive finalized observations within the same unlocked grant. An observation gap over one minute pauses the campaign, and time between grants does not count toward the 14-day goal. Each bot pauses independently after at least 14 active days and 10 finalized successful swaps when its after-fee marked value in the output token exceeds both its opening value and the unchanged-hold benchmark, and remains inside the approved drawdown limit. Reaching the loss limit pauses the bot earlier. Research is hypothetical and live outcomes depend on actual finalized transactions; neither result promises future profit.

Selecting an existing bot updates the URL to `/#/bots/my-bots?bot=<id>`, and its chart can be linked with `&chart=equity` or `&chart=backtest`. Bot IDs refer to records in that browser: sending the link does not publish a bot, its results, or its portfolio. A recipient without that record sees the bot workspace without an unrelated bot substituted. Research drafts, saved experiments, API keys, wallet data, signing consent, and running sessions are never serialized into links. A link can open the unsigned historical inspector; it cannot save, start, or resume a trading bot.

`navigation.ts` owns the bounded URL allowlist and canonical destinations. `BotsPage.vue` reads router state and passes public strategy/composer view choices to the research components; those components emit navigation changes without owning routing or execution authority.

## Start and test a strategy

**Strategy Lab** is the default workspace. Select an input token, one or more output tokens, and the strategy families to compare. The batch preview shows the exact experiment count before running; one batch supports up to 36 studies, with three animated comparisons visible at once. Results keep their settings, real market observations, directional fee evidence, validation results, and original virtual capital. You can shortlist studies, reopen their evidence, duplicate settings, and create a fresh idle paper bot from a chosen result. Opening **My Bots** preserves the research workspace. See [the Strategy Lab guide](bot-strategy-lab.md) for processing, persistence, and component contracts.

Use the **AI strategy assistant** to turn a written idea into a reviewed deterministic configuration. **Use Codex app** opens the user's desktop app with the current idea. Scoped website tools or **Copy instructions** provide the full public context for a draft, without an API key in Polkaswap. **Use an API key** retains OpenAI, Claude and custom provider connections with live model discovery. A generated configuration enters the queue only after **Add experiment**. Exact reviewed amount, interval, threshold, direction, and moving-average windows are preserved throughout historical evaluation and validation. Provider keys remain in a revocable memory closure and are never saved with experiments or bots; the Codex path receives no ChatGPT credentials. See [the composer guide](strategy-composer.md) and [Codex setup](codex-strategies.md) for supported rules, access and data handling.

The **Backtesting** tab retains the detailed single-strategy workspace and displays live calculation checkpoints. The **Backtesting** workspace lets users select their capital and traded tokens and research scheduled accumulation, price thresholds, and moving-average crossings before creating a bot. Adjust capital, order size, cooldown, strategy windows, slippage, and the XOR fee reserve, then run a study. Network and swapping fees are read from the current SORA chain for both trade directions and displayed as observations, with their block number. The results show the portfolio simulation alongside every evaluated trade candidate, its passed or failed constraints, and its retrospective outcome. Selecting a ball or a trade row explains the decision. This runs in the browser without connecting a wallet, authorizing trades, or saving a bot; reduced-motion preferences show decisions without animated travel.

Studies request real historical market data from **March 1, 2026 00:00 UTC** through the latest complete hour. The production workspace offers no generated sample prices or unverified CSV import. A shorter verified history is tested over its actual observed dates, with missing requested buckets included in coverage. An incompatible denomination, entirely unavailable history, unavailable current fee query, or disconnected chain leaves results unavailable. It never replaces a failed historical request with generated prices. Coverage and source provenance remain visible.

The bundled historical archive reconstructs hourly spot prices from actual SORA XYK pool reserves. Each observation records its chain block hash, state timestamp, following block timestamp, raw reserves and denomination evidence. These are real historical market observations. The animated trades are strategy decisions simulated against those prices; they are not a replay of recorded user transactions.

Advanced research queries indexed direct XOR-pool evidence across the full selected range first. Bundled archive observations and bounded historical RPC repair fill only genuine missing hours; they cannot overwrite corrected indexed rows. All combined pair and fee marks use the same direct-pool basis, with actual coverage and source provenance retained. See [history loading](bot-history-loading.md) for source priority, validation and cache behavior.

The profit distribution includes both selected and excluded candidates. Every decision candle with a following execution candle contributes one ball: a buy opportunity for ordinary or no-signal bars, or the strategy's sell when it signals a sale. Gates show signal, cooldown, balance, trade ceiling, and fee budget. Candidate outcomes compare the proposed fill with holding its input until the common final close, after entry swap fees, slippage, and one XOR network fee; they do not invent a final closing transaction. The final candle cannot contribute a new fill without a later execution price. Retrospective outcomes explain which positive or negative price moves the configured constraints selected or excluded, and never become decision inputs. Excluded opportunities can overlap, so their totals are not additional holdings, guaranteed missed income, or an achievable portfolio return. The portfolio simulation separately enforces sequential available capital and fees.

The distribution displays decisions as the evaluator computes them. Each actual checkpoint carries scope-specific checks and gate totals, newly evaluated study candidates, and exact partial portfolio metrics, fees, and selected/excluded outcomes. The engine yields across browser paints or worker presentation acknowledgements; no candidate is sent to the live distribution before its decision is evaluated. Coordinates and illustrative motion never determine trade decisions or money. Completed study results freeze immediately, without an automatic after-result replay. Restored studies are static. Reduced motion changes visual presentation, while metrics always reflect actual processed work. Every completed candidate remains inspectable in the distribution and paginated ledger.

The particle layer progressively uses WebGL2 when the browser accepts `failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat`. `tradeParticleRenderer.ts` batches every ball, outlined exclusion, trail and focus ring into one instanced-quad draw, using reusable buffers and an active divisor-zero corner attribute. Canvas2D continues to draw the measuring grid, histogram and inspected route and owns keyboard/pointer accessibility; the transparent GPU overlay cannot receive input. Both paths use the same CPU positions and actual calculation checkpoints. No financial arithmetic runs in shaders. Backing resolution is capped at twice logical resolution and unchanged dimensions are retained between frames. Unsupported contexts, failed program/buffer initialization, first-draw errors and context loss immediately fall back to Canvas2D at the current calculation state. Resources and listeners are released on fallback or unmount. No new libraries, network requests or deployment services are required.

Renderer tests cover complete 10,000-candidate batches, primitive style/order, buffer reuse, capability and shader failures, allocation/draw errors, context loss, disposal and bounded resizing. Component regressions verify keyboard inspection, reduced motion and unchanged accounting through fallback. The nonvisual `data-renderer` attribute is available for browser verification. See [WebGL best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices) and [the WebGL2 enabled-attribute requirement](https://registry.khronos.org/webgl/specs/2.0/#6.1).

Use **Create bot** to put the researched configuration in **My bots**. The new bot starts idle in Paper mode with its original virtual capital: no simulated trades, gains, paid fees, activity, equity, wallet identity, provider credentials, or active session are copied into its ledger. Historical studies retain their tested absolute threshold and deterministic strategy settings. New bots derive the output-token ceiling from that current quote, while retaining the configured input-token ceiling, slippage, price-impact limit, and fee reserve. The saved research summary retains observed buy and sell fees and their chain provenance separately from the new trading ledger. An unavailable current quote prevents saving. No wallet or API key is needed.

Network fees always debit XOR. When XOR is the capital token, the entered capital includes the fee reserve. With another capital token, the reserve is a separate XOR allocation; if XOR is the traded token, its initial holding contains that reserve exactly once. Historical observations for non-XOR capital must include `feeClose`, the price of one XOR in capital-token units, to value fees and the portfolio consistently. Simulation reduces swap output by both the observed swap fee and configured slippage. Actual unsigned quotes already contain route fees, so the backtest's swap-fee assumption is not charged again in paper/live execution.

The saved bot also retains a compact creation-study snapshot: source, period, coverage, validation settings, optimization flag, network- and swap-fee assumptions, and full-period hypothetical return, drawdown, and trade count. This snapshot records the original study, is separate from later paper/live performance, and is not proof that subsequent edits were backtested. **New bot** retains the full allocation, strategy, consent, and execution controls for manual creation.

1. Create or save a bot, choose its token pair, input-token capital, XOR fee allocation, and strategy. Every new bot starts in **Paper** mode with virtual holdings.
2. Set the order amount, interval, per-token trade ceilings, slippage, price-impact ceiling, and session duration. Sessions default to one hour; supported durations are one minute through 24 hours.
3. Use **Backtest** for completed historical candles or **Start** for paper trading with current unsigned Polkaswap quotes. New manually created and researched bots default to one block, nominally six seconds. Existing saved hour-based configurations preserve their interval. Threshold strategies buy below or sell above the configured price after their cooldown. Moving-average strategies trade on crossings of completed hourly closes, using the configured fast and slow windows. Block-level evaluation does not make hourly SMA observations more frequent. Rules strategies act only on a new completed hourly close. Between closes a rules bot skips block evaluations and is quoted and revalued at most once a minute (`REVALUE_INTERVAL_MS`); the first block that brings a new close is evaluated at once.

A running bot's activity log keeps 200 entries. Consecutive identical holds share one entry whose time is refreshed, so quiet evaluations do not push trades out of the log. The equity chart keeps 1,000 points, spaced by `equitySpacingMs` (at least a minute; the session length divided by 1,000 for longer sessions), so it spans the whole configured session. Each spacing window keeps its latest valuation, and the first point is always retained because **Net P&L**, return and drawdown are measured from it.
4. To trade real tokens, stop the bot, select **Live**, review and save its settings, then review the consent screen and authorize the session. A built-in wallet asks for the existing account's password. An external wallet retains its own signing prompts.

**Buy** spends the first asset to acquire the second; **Sell** spends the second asset to acquire the first. Prices express one second-asset token in first-asset units. The configured deterministic order amount is in the first asset. For a sell, the engine converts that amount at the observed price and caps it at the bot's available holdings; the separate sell-token ceiling still applies. An AI proposal's amount always denotes the actual input token for its chosen direction.

The workspace shows holdings, net P&L, drawdown, network fees, completed swaps, model usage, and trade or skipped-action reasons. Paper results are virtual, and backtest results are hypothetical. Live balances and settlement use finalized events. Performance is reported without promising income or future returns.

The token selectors use the complete whitelist, including tokens outside the common SDK asset set. Eligibility requires a positive target reserve and strictly more than one XOR in the current finalized XOR pool; XOR itself is included as the base token when eligible pools exist. Raw current reserve units are compared exactly, without applying the denomination coefficient a second time. Public liquidity discovery refreshes every 30 seconds.

Active bot sessions subscribe to finalized-block notifications. The scheduler coalesces arrivals during an active evaluation and never overlaps execution. A one-second maintenance timer reconnects subscriptions and supplies bounded polling when subscriptions are unavailable; missed trades are not replayed after sleep. Quote freshness, minimum six-second strategy intervals, session expiry, allocations, and finalized settlement remain enforced.

## Existing wallet and reusable allocation

An allocation is **reusable trading capital enforced by this application**. It is not an on-chain allowance, escrow, transfer to a bot wallet, or permission which other applications must respect. For example, 100 allocated XOR can buy VAL; that VAL belongs to the same bot's virtual portfolio and can later be sold for XOR and reinvested. Wallet deposits and unrelated transfers never enlarge that virtual portfolio automatically.

Fee capital is included in allocated holdings. The manual **New bot** form treats trade capital and the fee allowance as separate entries: 100 XOR trade capital plus a 1 XOR allowance reserves 101 XOR. The research workspace instead treats entered XOR capital as the total including its fee reserve, and preserves that total when creating the bot. Each successful swap subtracts the actual input and finalized XOR fee and adds the actual output. A failed on-chain transaction still consumes its actual fee. The cumulative fee allowance and per-input-token trade ceilings apply across the portfolio's recorded activity.

`allocation.ts` protects the unspent fee reserve (`feeBudgetCodec - feesPaidCodec`) in strategy sizing, research, paper fills, proposal/quote validation, and the atomic live order ledger. XOR bought by a bot becomes tradable only above that floor; the initial reserve cannot trigger a sell. XOR-input buys keep their configured size and wait when trade capital is insufficient. Actual paid fees reduce both holdings and the protected reserve once, leaving earned XOR available without reserving the current fee twice. Underfunded legacy allocations are rejected; swap proceeds cannot finance their own fee. The passive buy benchmark preserves the same reserve while paying its actual entry fee.

Running, paused, and attention-state live bots retain their allocations. **Pause** immediately revokes this tab's signing session. **Stop** also releases the allocation once unresolved transactions have been reconciled. Tokens stay in the existing wallet. Stop does not sell the bot's holdings. If a signed transaction may already have been submitted, its reservation stays in place until finality or proven expiry; the UI reports that pending state instead of making the same tokens available twice.

Before every live trade, current transferable wallet balances must cover the sum of active allocations. Manual spending which invalidates that condition suspends execution. A second bot cannot reserve overlapping capital. A second tab cannot become the live executor for the same account and network while the first owns its browser lock. After reload, persisted strategies and transaction records are available but no bot resumes automatically.

## Session and signing boundary

`live.ts` owns private signing closures. For SORA and Google Drive-backed built-in accounts, it constructs a private instance from the existing encrypted keyring JSON and unlocks that instance with the user's password. It never inserts that instance into the shared keyring, changes the ordinary wallet signer, disables wallet confirmations, or places signing material in Pinia, saved strategies, model requests, or the public agent API. Locking discards the private instance and its signing references; JavaScript does not guarantee physical erasure of every historical memory copy.

The only constructed transaction is the SDK-equivalent `liquidityProxy.swap` call with an exact desired input and minimum output. It is validated against the current session, approved pair, per-token amount ceiling, slippage, price impact, XOR fee allowance, current account/network/runtime, and the prepare-issued quote and call digests. The adapter revalidates immediately before signing and again after external-wallet prompts, before broadcast. The signer closure is not exported.

The order lifecycle is `reserved → signed → submitted → confirmed/failed`. Input and maximum fee reservations are committed atomically in IndexedDB. The signed transaction hash and signing block are committed before broadcast. An uncertain broadcast blocks further orders for that account. Reconciliation searches finalized blocks for that exact hash and parses the corresponding swap and fee events; an absent mortal transaction is released only after its complete expiry window has passed. Missing archival receipt data leaves the reservation pending.

Live trading requires working IndexedDB and the Web Locks API. Storage errors stop execution. There is one scheduler per tab and one live executor per account/network across tabs. An unlocked session continues in a background tab. Stop, expiry, wallet/network changes, reload/page exit, offline events, and detected sleep revoke signing. Initial authorization must happen in a visible tab; hiding while it is pending cancels that start. Resuming a revoked session requires a fresh unlock, market data and reconciliation. V1 has no background service worker or always-on remote execution.

## OpenAI, Claude, and custom connections

Enter a provider, explicit model ID, and API key in **Connect AI**. Credentials remain in a revocable memory closure and are cleared from the input immediately; they are never saved to IndexedDB, strategy exports, logs, or static assets. Stop, disconnect/replacement, and page disposal revoke the connection. Each bot requires its own explicit connection. Do not put credentials in strategy instructions or custom endpoint URLs.

- OpenAI uses [Responses](https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/responses/methods/create) with `store: false`, a strict JSON schema, and a 1,500-token output cap.
- Claude uses [Messages](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/messages) with one named structured tool result and a 1,500-token output cap. Browser requests set the provider's explicit direct-browser-access header.
- Custom bots receive the same limited context over an explicitly connected HTTPS endpoint. Redirects, URL credentials, query strings, and fragments are rejected. The endpoint must support browser CORS for the deployed Polkaswap origin and `Content-Type` plus optional `Authorization` headers. Requests omit cookies and referrers. CORS configuration belongs to the endpoint operator; the static app supplies no proxy.

Models receive the selected pair, the bot's holdings, selected limits, strategy instructions, and up to 120 market observations. They do not receive the wallet's identity, unrelated holdings, or signing keys. The only accepted trade result has exactly `action`, `amount`, and `reason`. Deterministic code applies every spending check, regardless of provider advice. Invalid responses, authentication errors, stale data, timeouts, or breached limits suspend execution.

Requests are serialized per connection and limited to 30 seconds and 32 KiB of response data. Direct AI strategies follow their configured block evaluation interval, including one-block intervals; the scheduler does not overlap requests. Whole-block intervals count finalized block heights using session-local evaluation/fill markers, so quote latency does not skip the next eligible block. A successful fill anchors its cooldown to the latest observed finalized head, and duplicate/regressing heads are ignored. Historical non-block-aligned intervals and offline polling keep elapsed-time semantics. Quotes, fills and signatures keep their actual timestamps. Deterministic strategy generation has a separate 60-second cooldown. Reported input/output token counts and successful request counts, including strategy suggestions, are shown separately from network fees; these are provider-reported usage units, not a billing estimate. Provider charges may also apply to failed requests.

AI can suggest a deterministic strategy for review. The user must apply its configuration explicitly before testing it. Historical testing never replays present-day model calls as if they were historical decisions.

The simple GO flow's desktop training contract uses `signalTiming: "closed-hour"` for `sma` and `signalTiming: null` for `dca`, `threshold` and `rules`. Its response schema expresses those kind-specific values, and both native website tools and the portable form reject `live-price`. All simple-flow evaluation uses completed hourly observations; the advanced composer's explicit live-price option is separate.

### Typed HTTPS endpoint example

The public integration types are in [`provider-protocol.ts`](../src/features/bot-trading/provider-protocol.ts). A minimal proposal handler is in [`examples/bot-provider.ts`](examples/bot-provider.ts); it deliberately returns Hold until the operator adds strategy logic. Host that handler in your own authenticated HTTPS service with appropriate CORS; it is not a server runtime shipped by Polkaswap.

The browser sends:

```ts
import type { BotProviderRequest, BotProviderResponse } from '../src/features/bot-trading/provider-protocol';

// POST /polkaswap-proposal
// Content-Type: application/json
// Authorization: Bearer <optional session credential>
// Body: BotProviderRequest { version: 1, task, context, responseSchema }

const response: BotProviderResponse = {
  proposal: { action: 'hold', amount: '0', reason: 'No configured trading signal.' },
  usage: { input_tokens: 0, output_tokens: 0 },
};
```

For `task: 'strategy'`, return `{ strategy: StrategyConfig }` with a deterministic `dca`, `threshold`, or `sma` kind. Returning code, transfers, extra proposal properties, tool commands, wallet fields, or modified consent cannot authorize those operations.

## Historical data and assumptions

The bundled `public/bot-history/sora-mainnet-hourly-2026-03-01.json` contains 4,729 hourly observations from March 1 01:00 UTC to September 14 01:00 UTC (the close of each completed hourly bucket). The seven assets in that bundled capture are XOR, VAL, PSWAP, DAI, ETH, XSTUSD and XST; all 42 directed pairs have complete historical spot-price coverage in this capture. The parser validates the recorded chain, block bounds, asset precision and denomination before constructing pair and XOR fee valuations from the same state. The page compares that chain identity and denomination with the current fee snapshot before running. The My Bots backtest dialog uses the same March-onward loader and checks.

To extend the immutable archive, run `node scripts/bots/extract-archive-history.mjs` with the repository dependencies installed. The extractor makes bounded, read-only requests to the approved archive and resumes `output/bot-history/archive-hours.jsonl`. It never configures a node or indexer. Rebuild the static site after updating the dataset. See `public/bot-history/README.md` for source details. The browser prioritizes indexed direct-pool evidence for the full range and uses the bundled capture only for missing hours, including when a pair is absent from the capture. A study does not fail just because its first verified price is later than March 1; actual available dates and coverage are retained. Unavailable or incompatible hours remain missing. A September 14, 2026 browser verification found 22 supported XOR/KUSD hourly closes spanning September 13 17:00 UTC–September 14 14:00 UTC; older rows without compatible denomination evidence were excluded.

`history.ts` reads raw decimal-string `AssetSnapshot.priceUSD.close` values from the configured indexer. It aligns completed hourly or daily buckets for both pair assets and XOR where needed. Missing, invalid, duplicated, unaligned, or unsupported buckets remain missing; values are never filled forward. Queries use the production indexer's 100-row page limit and are bounded to 365 days, 10,000 observations, and 100 pages per asset.

Archive repairs can provide a verified completed close without the hour's open, high, low, volume, supply, mint or burn. Bot history can use that close with its chain evidence. The price-chart reader requires actual, consistent OHLC and observed volume; the supply-chart reader requires observed supply, mint and burn. They skip incomplete or invalid rows instead of synthesizing candles or treating unknown fields as zero. Observed zero volume and flows remain valid.

Historical simulation observes a completed close and executes the resulting signal at the **next available candle close**, applying configured slippage and the current observed network and swap fees for the corresponding buy or sell direction. No fill is made from the final candle's signal. This avoids same-candle lookahead but does not reconstruct historical routing, execution liquidity or price impact. Coverage reports the available fraction of requested buckets. Gaps remain visible and can delay a simulated fill. Historical data stays at its observed hourly/daily resolution: configuring a six-second strategy never interpolates prices or invents sub-hour fills.

`research-fees.ts` pins observations to one finalized block of the connected SORA chain. It queries `liquidityProxy.quote` for the actual tested input amount and the reverse amount received, selects the best returned route, and reads the explicit per-asset fee map. Input-token fees are measured against input; output-token fees are measured against gross output. It builds unsigned fee-estimation envelopes and calls the pinned runtime's `transactionPaymentApi.queryInfo`; nothing is signed with a wallet or broadcast. The current XOR fee is decoded directly from chain codec units, with no invented denomination scaling. Both directions retain their own network and proportional swap fees. There is no hardcoded or cached fee fallback.

For routes such as VAL → XOR → PSWAP, the swap fee is charged in the intermediary XOR token. The loader reproduces both legs at the full quote's exact finalized block and DEX. Each leg must have the expected direct route, and their final output and combined XOR fee must exactly match the full quote, down to a single codec unit. If the first leg returns net XOR amount `x`, with first-leg fee `f1` and second-leg fee `f2`, the combined fee fraction is `(f1 + f2) / (x + f1)`. This uses the actual route's intermediate amounts without introducing an additional conversion swap or its price impact. The fee percentage and equivalent capital deduction are rounded upward once using integer arithmetic. The observation retains the raw XOR fee, both leg fees, gross and net intermediary amounts, equivalent capital deduction, and block provenance. The reverse route is verified independently. Unsupported fee assets or a mismatch in this decomposition leave fees unavailable.

The observations expire five minutes after retrieval. By default, `createResearchFeeLoader().load(bot, settings)` also requires a finalized-state timestamp no older than five minutes; live setup and promotion retain this strict path. Historical simulations explicitly pass `{ allowHistoricalFinalizedState: true }` as the third argument so a delayed finalization does not prevent analysis of historical candles. This changes only the finalized state's permitted age: every quote and payment estimate still uses the exact finalized hash, valid chain timestamps and matching denomination; request deadlines, connection/runtime identity checks and retrieval expiry still apply. The observation and saved study retain `finalizedAt`, displayed with the block as the dated fee basis. Invalid or excessively future timestamps fail in both modes. Historical fee calibration is never substituted for the fresh quote required by live execution. Changes to the token pair, notional or slippage policy require new fee observations. Current fees are a calibration applied to historical simulated trades; they are not represented as the fees paid at past blocks. Route selection, amounts, runtime parameters and actual future fees can change. On September 14, 2026, finalized block 27,642,604 returned `0.100020612589707326 XOR` for the checked 10 XOR → VAL swap and its reverse; its forward quote explicitly returned a `0.06 XOR` swap fee. A separate observation at block 27,647,467 verified both legs of 10 VAL → PSWAP and the reverse using the exact PSWAP output. These are dated verification observations, not default fee constants; their raw evidence and derived fee rates are recorded in the [validation report](reports/bot-backtesting-validation.md).

Research supports full-period inspection, a chronological train/test holdout, and walk-forward validation. Training occupies the first 50–80% of available candles; walk-forward uses 2–5 subsequent disjoint test periods and expands its training window as earlier observations become available. A one-candle gap separates each train/test boundary. Every test starts with fresh capital and zero strategy state, including a new moving-average warmup; its absolute threshold remains anchored to the first training observation. Fold returns therefore describe independent test portfolios rather than one continuously compounded account.

Optional training compares at most three nearby configurations of the chosen deterministic method, selects the best training return with exact decimal comparisons, and evaluates that fixed choice on the later test period. Ties retain the current settings. The main full-period study retains the user's chosen configuration. Applying the latest training recommendation explicitly opens a new full-period replay with validation and parameter search disabled: later walk-forward training includes earlier test windows, so their scores cannot be reused as unseen tests for that final choice. Training and test results remain visibly distinct in the original cross-validation run. Repeatedly tuning against visible test results makes those results part of model selection; another untouched period is needed for an independent assessment. Cross-validation results remain hypothetical and cannot establish future performance.

The optional indexer `denominator` field records the chain coefficient at each snapshot's actual close. Only buckets compatible with the current finalized coefficient are used. Older indexers can use archival chain evidence, or a current cumulative coefficient of one, to establish that no denomination transition occurred. If that proof is unavailable, historical simulation and live/paper moving-average signals are unavailable. Current quote-based scheduled, threshold, and AI strategies remain independent of historical indexer availability.

The companion indexer change and rollout evidence are documented in `polkaswap-indexer/docs/bot-history.md`. This frontend does not treat missing metadata as a verified historical series or silently normalize unknown transitions.

### Visible computation motion

Both research views animate each newly evaluated batch through the constraint gates into the distribution. `TradeDistribution.waitForCheckpoint` and the bounded presentation coordinator let a visible lane acknowledge the batch after its last motion frame; workers continue when the actual view has presented their result. The lab routes acknowledgements by run ID, so parallel lanes do not block one another. Hidden or reduced-motion views release immediately. The final result has no automatic replay queue.
